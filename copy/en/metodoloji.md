**Meta description:**
How we score TRNC insurance companies on six observable measures, which sources we use, and why

---

## Summary

**H2:** In short

We score six measures and leave two blank. Everything we score is **externally verifiable**:
what a company publishes, how many classes it sells products in, where it can be reached, and
whether its website works. What we leave blank — **financial strength and claims payment
performance** — has not been published company by company in the TRNC since 2016. We do not
guess.

---

## Section — First: company or agent?

**H2:** First: is it a company or an agent?

Some names that appear in online lists of "TRNC insurance companies" are agents or brokers, not
insurance companies. An insurance company issues the policy, carries the risk and pays the
claim. An agent sells the policy.

We score companies. Agents and brokers are not in the list, and the service an agent gives you
is not part of the company's score.

---

## Section — The six measures

**H2:** What we measure, where from, and why

For each measure: what we measure · source · why.

**Transparency — 25%**
*What we measure:* whether the company publishes a corporate email address, an open postal
address, telephone numbers and its policy conditions.
*Source:* the company's own website, tested directly.
*Why:* what a company publishes is the precondition for verifying everything else.

**Product breadth — 20%**
*What we measure:* how many classes of insurance the company actually offers products in.
*Source:* the company's own product pages.
*Why:* it shows whether a single company can cover your needs.

**Accessibility — 20%**
*What we measure:* agency network, offices outside Lefkoşa, telephone and WhatsApp lines.
*Source:* the company's own website and published contact details.
*Why:* when you have a claim, being able to reach the company matters more than the price.

**Digital service — 20%**
*What we measure:* whether the website works and what can be done on it.
*Source:* direct testing, July 2026.
*Why:* a working website is the channel most readers use to reach a company first.

**Foreign language — 10%**
*What we measure:* whether the company serves readers in any language other than Turkish.
*Source:* the company's own website.
*Why:* many people insured in the TRNC do not read Turkish.

**Corporate depth — 5%**
*What we measure:* whether the company is part of a banking group, or the branch of a company
from Türkiye.
*Source:* the company's own statements and the KKSRSB member list.
*Why:* it says something about the structure behind the company — but not much, which is why
it carries the least weight.

**Weights — why these weights:**
We distributed the weights according to what is of use to the reader at the moment of a claim.
The heaviest measure is transparency, because what a company publishes is the precondition for
verifying everything else. The lightest is corporate depth, because being part of a large group
is no guarantee of good service.

## Section — Measures we do not score

**H2:** The two measures we leave blank

**Financial strength.** Company-level equity and balance sheet data have not been published in
the TRNC since 2016.

**Claims payment performance.** No ranking of paid claims or loss ratios was found for any year.

These bands are empty for every company. They are not a shortcoming of any company. They are
the limit of what we can see.

## Section — Where we looked for financial data

**H2:** Where we looked for the financial data

| Where we looked | What we found |
|---|---|
| KKSRSB statistics archive, 2019–2026 | **No files at all** |
| KKSRSB archive, 2017–2018 | Consolidated balance sheet only — no company named |
| KKSRSB archive, 2016 | The last company-level data: premium ranking, outstanding claims ranking |
| KKSRSB archive, 2007 | The last market share data |
| Insurance Sector 2024 Annual Report | No company-level financial statements — the appendix is a company directory only |
| Websites of the 6 largest companies | None of the 6 published an annual report or financial statements |
| Stock exchange listing | In practice, none |
| Ranking by paid claims / loss ratio | **Not found for any year** |

**Below the table:**
The 2018 consolidated balance sheet PDF was regenerated on 4 February 2026. The archive has
been updated, but no new data has been added to it.

**One open end:**
Articles 43–47 of Law No. 60/2010 may impose a requirement to publish balance sheets in the
Official Gazette. That part of the law is not legible in the publicly available PDF text. We
will read it directly through the state printing office. If the requirement exists, company
balance sheets could be compiled from the Official Gazette archive and the financial strength
measure could come back.

## Section — How the score is calculated

**H2:** How the score is calculated

Each measure is scored on its own scale, then combined at the weights above: transparency 25%,
product breadth 20%, accessibility 20%, digital service 20%, foreign language 10%, corporate
depth 5%. Financial strength and claims payment performance carry no weight because they are
not scored.

---

## Section — What this score does not tell you

**H2:** What this score does not tell you

- **It does not tell you whether the company will pay your claim.** There is no data on payment
  performance.
- **It does not tell you whether the company is financially sound.** There is no equity data.
- **It does not tell you whether the price is reasonable.** The floor tariff narrows price
  differences in compulsory motor insurance; in kasko, prices cannot be compared directly
  because the scope of cover varies.
- **It does not measure customer satisfaction.** There is no systematic, verifiable complaints
  data.
- **It does not assess the agent.** The service given by the agent who sold you the policy is
  not part of the company's score.

**Closing sentence:**
> A company with a high score is one that does well the things an outside observer can verify.
> That is not proof that it is a good insurer — only as much as we know.

## Section — Why we do not count court decisions

**H2:** Why we do not count court and arbitration decisions

KKSRSB publishes a compilation of Supreme Court decisions, but there is no systematic index.
Counting how many decisions a company's name appears in may show that it sells a lot of
policies rather than that it loses a lot of cases. Because we cannot normalise for size, we do
not use this data.

## Section — What we are and what we are not

**H2:** What we are and what we are not

**We are not an insurance agent or broker.** We do not sell policies and we take no commission.

**We are not an official body.** Kuzey Kıbrıs Sigorta Bilgi Merkezi (KKSBM), the Department of
Money, Exchange and Development Fund Affairs, and KKSRSB are real institutions. This site is not
connected to them. It is an independent publication that uses the data they publish.

**We do not take payment for scores.** No company can pay to change its position in the list.

## Section — Updating and corrections

**H2:** Updating and corrections

**Updating.** Last updated July 2026. Company websites are retested and the scores are revised
at each update.

**Corrections.** If you find information incorrect, send it to us with the source. We will check
it and correct it.

TRNC comprehensive motor insurance (kasko)

Comprehensive motor insurance — known locally as kasko — covers damage to your own vehicle. Compulsory third-party motor insurance pays only for the damage you cause to the other party. These are two separate policies and neither replaces the other.

Common mistake

Having compulsory motor insurance does not mean your vehicle is insured

If the accident was your fault, only kasko repairs your vehicle.

This is the most common misunderstanding in the TRNC. The compulsory policy protects you from a penalty. It does not protect your vehicle.

Cover

What it covers and what it does not

✓ Covers

  • ·Collision and overturning — even where you are at fault
  • ·Fire and explosion
  • ·Theft and damage caused during an attempted theft
  • ·Natural disasters
  • ·Glass breakage — may be a separate cover depending on the policy

✕ Does not cover

  • ·Damage you cause to the other party — compulsory motor insurance is needed for that
  • ·Driving without a licence
  • ·Driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs
  • ·Racing and test driving

Which covers are standard in the policy and which are add-ons varies by company. KKSRSB publishes the kasko general conditions in Turkish and English — both are official texts, not translations. If you read English, use the English text directly.

Pricing

Kasko has a floor as well

As with compulsory motor insurance, a minimum premium is set for kasko. The sector report calls this “taban çarpma” — floor loading — and it is banded by vehicle value.

Because the scope of cover varies from company to company, two kasko policies at the same price can cover very different things. Comparing on price alone is misleading.

Market observation

A range of 300–500 € a year is reported for full kasko. That figure comes from a commercial source, not an official tariff; it varies with the value and age of the vehicle and the scope of cover.

Choosing a company

Kasko prices do differ, but price is not the only measure

In compulsory motor insurance the floor tariff pushes prices close together. In kasko the scope of cover varies, so the real difference is inside the policy.

Frequently asked

Short answers

+ Is kasko compulsory?

No. In the TRNC only third-party liability insurance is a legal requirement. However, if you bought the vehicle on credit or lease, the finance provider may require kasko under the contract.

+ The accident was the other driver's fault. Should I use my kasko?

The other party's compulsory motor insurance covers the repair of your vehicle. If you use your own kasko, your no-claims step may be affected. A step system is applied in the TRNC and the records are held at Kuzey Kıbrıs Sigorta Bilgi Merkezi (KKSBM), but the number of steps and the discount rates are not published in any public source, so we do not give a rate here. Ask the company that issued your policy.

+ Can I buy kasko at a border crossing?

No. The insurance kiosks in the buffer zone sell only compulsory third-party insurance. See the border crossing guide.

+ Where do I go if there is a dispute about a kasko claim?

You can apply to the Insurance Arbitration Commission within KKSRSB using its application form. The Republic of Cyprus Financial Ombudsman and EU consumer mechanisms do not apply in the TRNC.

+ Where can I read the kasko general conditions?

KKSRSB publishes them in Turkish and English. The English version is an official text, not a translation.

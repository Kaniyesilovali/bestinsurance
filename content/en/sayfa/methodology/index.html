What we measure, and what we cannot

If we give a company a score, we have to show where that score comes from. This page carries the full definition of the six measures, the source behind each one, and how the scores are combined — and why the two things people most want to know are the two we leave blank.

In short

Scope
39 non-life insurance companies, members of KKSRSB
Measures
6 scored, 2 not scored
Score range
0.0 – 10.0
Updating

First: is it a company or an agent?

Not every organisation operating under the word "sigorta" in the TRNC is an insurance company. Some are agents or brokers — intermediaries selling other companies' policies. They are not the risk carrier behind your policy.

We assess only licensed insurance companies that are members of KKSRSB (Kuzey Kıbrıs Sigorta ve Reasürans Şirketleri Birliği). Many online lists of "TRNC insurance companies" do not make this distinction and rank agents as if they were companies. If an organisation is not on the association's member list, it is not on this site either.

The scored measures

Six measures, every one verifiable from the outside

Each measure comes from a source you can reach as easily as we can. If you want to check a score, you should be able to go to the source and see the same thing.

  1. 01

    Transparency

    weight 25%

    What we measure

    Whether the company publishes a corporate email address, an open postal address, telephone numbers and its policy conditions.

    Source

    The company's own website, tested directly.

    Why

    What a company publishes is the precondition for verifying everything else.

  2. 02

    Product breadth

    weight 20%

    What we measure

    How many classes of insurance the company actually offers products in.

    Source

    The company's own product pages.

    Why

    It shows whether a single company can cover your needs.

  3. 03

    Accessibility

    weight 20%

    What we measure

    Agency network, offices outside Lefkoşa, telephone and WhatsApp lines.

    Source

    The company's own website and published contact details.

    Why

    When you have a claim, being able to reach the company matters more than the price.

  4. 04

    Digital service

    weight 20%

    What we measure

    Whether the website works and what can be done on it.

    Source

    Direct testing, July 2026.

    Why

    A working website is the channel most readers use to reach a company first.

  5. 05

    Foreign language

    weight 10%

    What we measure

    Whether the company serves readers in any language other than Turkish.

    Source

    The company's own website.

    Why

    Many people insured in the TRNC do not read Turkish.

  6. 06

    Corporate depth

    weight 5%

    What we measure

    Whether the company is part of a banking group, or the branch of a company from Türkiye.

    Source

    The company's own statements and the KKSRSB member list.

    Why

    It says something about the structure behind the company — but not much, which is why it carries the least weight.

Why these weights. They follow what is of use to the reader at the moment of a claim. The heaviest measure is transparency, because what a company publishes is the precondition for verifying everything else. The lightest is corporate depth, because being part of a large group is no guarantee of good service.

The measures we do not score

The two measures we leave blank

Financial strength. Company-level equity and balance sheet data have not been published in the TRNC since 2016.

Claims payment performance. No ranking of paid claims or loss ratios was found for any year.

These bands are empty for every company. They are not a shortcoming of any company. They are the limit of what we can see.

One open end

Articles 43–47 of Law No. 60/2010 may impose a requirement to publish balance sheets in the Official Gazette. That part of the law is not legible in the publicly available PDF text. We will read it directly through the state printing office. If the requirement exists, company balance sheets could be compiled from the Official Gazette archive and the financial strength measure could come back.

How the score is calculated

Each measure is scored on its own scale, then combined at the weights above: transparency 25%, product breadth 20%, accessibility 20%, digital service 20%, foreign language 10%, corporate depth 5%. Financial strength and claims payment performance carry no weight, because they are not scored. Where a measure could not be collected for a particular company, it does not count as zero — its weight is distributed across the remaining measures.

What this score does not tell you

  • It does not tell you whether the company will pay your claim. There is no data on payment performance.
  • It does not tell you whether the company is financially sound. There is no equity data.
  • It does not tell you whether the price is reasonable. The floor tariff narrows price differences in compulsory motor insurance; in kasko, prices cannot be compared directly because the scope of cover varies.
  • It does not measure customer satisfaction. There is no systematic, verifiable complaints data.
  • It does not assess the agent. The service given by the agent who sold you the policy is not part of the company's score.
A company with a high score is one that does well the things an outside observer can verify. That is not proof that it is a good insurer — only as much as we know.

Why we do not count court and arbitration decisions

KKSRSB publishes a compilation of Supreme Court decisions, but there is no systematic index. Counting how many decisions a company's name appears in may show that it sells a lot of policies rather than that it loses a lot of cases. Because we cannot normalise for size, we do not use this data.

What we are and what we are not

We are not an insurance agent or broker. We do not sell policies and we take no commission.

We are not an official body. Kuzey Kıbrıs Sigorta Bilgi Merkezi (KKSBM), the Department of Money, Exchange and Development Fund Affairs, and KKSRSB are real institutions. This site is not connected to them. It is an independent publication that uses the data they publish.

We do not take payment for scores. No company can pay to change its position in the list.

Updating and corrections

Updating. Last updated July 2026. Company websites are retested and the scores revised at each update.

Corrections. If you find information incorrect, send it to us with the source TR. We will check it and correct it.
